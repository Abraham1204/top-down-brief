# Professional tone

The aim: the reader trusts the writer, understands the message on first reading, and knows what to do. Structure does most of the work; wording does the rest.

## Principles

1. **Plain over grand.** Use the everyday word: "use" not "utilize", "help" not "facilitate", "start" not "commence", "about" not "with regard to".
2. **Specific over vague.** Numbers, dates, names and owners beat adjectives. "Sales fell 12% in Q3" not "sales were somewhat disappointing".
3. **Confident, not overclaiming.** State conclusions directly ("We recommend...") without hedging chains ("It might perhaps be worth considering whether..."). Do not overstate certainty either; qualify only once, where it matters.
4. **Courteous, not servile.** One polite phrase is enough. Avoid stacked apologies and gratitude ("So sorry to bother you, I really appreciate..., thanks so much in advance").
5. **Active voice by default.** "Finance approved the budget", not "the budget was approved". Use passive only when the actor is unknown or irrelevant, or to avoid blaming someone.
6. **Neutral and respectful.** No sarcasm, exclamation marks, emojis, slang, internal jokes or emotionally loaded words in official material. Inclusive, gender-neutral language.
7. **Concrete images.** Turn abstract noun-strings into subjects doing things: "We must redeploy the sales force to match the new trading environment", not "the enhancement of cost-effectiveness via sales force realignment initiatives".
8. **One idea per sentence, one topic per paragraph.** Sentences averaging 15–20 words; paragraphs of 2–4 sentences in email.

## Wording swaps

| Avoid | Prefer |
|---|---|
| I just wanted to reach out to... | I am writing to... / (just start with the point) |
| Please find attached | I have attached / Attached is |
| Per my last email | As I mentioned on [date] |
| At this point in time | Now |
| In order to | To |
| Due to the fact that | Because |
| We will endeavour to | We will |
| Going forward | From [date] / In future |
| Kindly revert | Please reply |
| Do the needful | Please [specific action] |
| ASAP | by [day, date, time] |
| Let me know your thoughts | Please confirm X by [date] / Do you agree with Y? |
| Hope this helps | (cut) |
| Synergies, leverage, circle back, touch base, bandwidth | Specific words for what is meant |

## Hedges and fillers to cut

just, actually, basically, really, very, quite, somewhat, perhaps, I think, I feel, I believe (unless opinion must be marked), sort of, kind of, needless to say, it goes without saying, as you know.

## Register by reader

| Reader | Greeting | Close | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Senior executive / board | Dear [Name] / [Name], | Regards / Best regards | Shortest possible; decision first |
| External client or partner | Dear [Name], | Kind regards / Best regards | Slightly warmer; no internal jargon or acronyms |
| Regulator / government / legal | Dear [Title Surname], | Yours sincerely / Yours faithfully | Most formal; precise; no contractions |
| Colleague / team (official) | Hi [Name] / Hi all, | Thanks / Best | Contractions acceptable |
| All-staff announcement | Dear colleagues / Hi everyone, | [Name, title] | Clear what changes for them, and when |

## Tone red flags to catch in review

- Blame or defensiveness ("As I already explained...", "Obviously...").
- Passive-aggressive phrases ("Per my last email", "As previously stated", "Friendly reminder").
- Commands without courtesy to external or senior readers.
- Over-familiarity with someone the writer does not know well.
- Unexplained acronyms or internal project names for external readers.
- Mixed spelling conventions within one piece.
- Promises or commitments the writer may not be authorized to make: flag these to the user rather than removing them.
