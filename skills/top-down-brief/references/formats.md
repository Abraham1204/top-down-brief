# Formats by piece type

Every format below is the same pyramid: answer first, grouped support, evidence, ask. Only the packaging changes.

## Email

```
Subject: [Action or conclusion in the subject: e.g. "Approval needed: Shift Q4 ad budget to Meta"]

Dear / Hi [Name],

[Bottom line: the conclusion, recommendation or request in 1–2 sentences.]

[Optional one-line context, only if the reader needs reminding.]

[Key arguments: 2–3 numbered points, each a full statement with a short label.]
1. [Label]: [point]
2. [Label]: [point]
3. [Label]: [point]

[Supporting details: brief bullets of data, facts, metrics. Skippable by a reader who already trusts the conclusion.]
- Data: ...
- Action: Please [specific action] by [day, date] so that [consequence].

[Courteous close]
[Name]
[Title, if external]
```

Worked example:

> **Subject:** Approval needed: Shift Q4 ad budget to Meta
>
> Hi [Name],
>
> We should reallocate 30% of our search ad budget to Meta platforms for Q4 to maximize ROI.
>
> 1. **Higher conversion rates:** Meta campaigns yielded a 24% lower cost-per-acquisition last month.
> 2. **Audience fatigue on Google:** Our search impressions dropped 12% over the last two quarters.
> 3. **Creative readiness:** The design team has already produced high-performing video assets.
>
> - **Data:** CPA on Meta dropped to $14.20 vs $18.60 on Search.
> - **Action:** Please reply "Approved" by Thursday so we can reconfigure the campaigns.
>
> Best,
> [Your Name]

Subject line rules:
- Lead with a tag when the reader must act: `Approval needed:`, `Decision needed:`, `Action required:`, `FYI:`, `Update:`.
- Then the message, not the topic. Aim for under ~60 characters where possible.

Length: aim for one screen. If the email needs more than ~250 words of body, consider moving detail into an attachment and keeping the email as the pyramid's top two levels.

## Memo / briefing note

```
To / From / Date / Subject (subject states the conclusion)

[Opening paragraph: S-C in 1–3 sentences, then the answer.]

[One heading per key-line point, each heading a full statement in parallel form.]
  [1–2 paragraphs or a few bullets of support under each.]

Next steps / Decision required
  [Numbered actions with owner and date.]
```

## Formal letter (external: clients, regulators, partners)

- Letterhead, date, recipient, reference line if applicable, salutation.
- First paragraph: purpose and main point ("I am writing to confirm that...", "We are pleased to propose...").
- Middle: key-line points as short paragraphs, one idea each.
- Final paragraph: the ask or next step, and a named contact.
- Close: "Yours sincerely" when the recipient is named; "Yours faithfully" when not (British convention); "Sincerely" in US usage. Follow `house-style.md`.

## Report / board or management paper

```
Title (states the conclusion where possible)
Executive summary (the top two levels of the pyramid, ≤ 1 page)
  - S-C-Q in a short paragraph
  - Governing thought / recommendation
  - Key-line points, each one sentence
  - Decisions requested
1. [Key-line point 1 as a heading statement]
   1.1 ... (numbered sub-points, fewer than two paragraphs of support each where possible)
2. [Key-line point 2]
3. [Key-line point 3]
Next steps (owners, dates)
Appendices (data, alternatives considered, methodology)
```

- Heading hierarchy mirrors the pyramid; headings at the same level use parallel wording.
- Do not number the introduction or the summary; they are not points in the argument.
- For long documents, open each section with a one-line reminder of how it links to the governing thought, and summarize before moving on.

## Proposal

Use the proposal pattern in `introductions.md`. Standard headings (Background, Objectives, Approach, Work plan, Deliverables, Team, Fees) are fine as containers, but the opening page must state the problem, the proposed approach, and why it will work, as a pyramid.

## Status / progress update

```
Subject: [Project]: [one-line status, e.g. "On track for 14 Nov launch; 1 decision needed"]

Status: [On track / At risk / Off track], [one sentence why]
Decisions needed: [numbered, with deadline] (put these first; they're why the reader opens it)
Progress: [key-line points by workstream or by importance]
Risks / issues: [each with impact and mitigation]
Next milestones: [date – milestone]
```

## Announcement (internal or external)

- Headline/subject states what is happening.
- First sentence: what, who is affected, when.
- Then: why (briefly), what it means for the reader, what they need to do (if anything).
- Close: where to get more information / who to contact.

## Text slides

- Slide title = a full-sentence statement of the slide's point, not a label.
- One idea per slide; about six lines or 30 words maximum.
- The sequence of slide titles, read alone, should tell the whole argument (the key line).
- For exhibit slides (charts), the title states what the chart proves ("Western region accounts for 60% of growth").
- Begin with the introduction slide(s) (S-C-Q + answer), then one section per key-line point.
